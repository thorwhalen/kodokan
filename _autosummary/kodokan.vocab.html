kodokan.vocab#

Word parts of technique names: which techniques share which part, and how often.

A Kodokan technique name is a compound of recurring word parts: Harai-goshi is harai (sweep) + goshi (hip), O-soto-gari is o (large) + soto (outer) + gari (reap). Learning a dozen of those parts makes a hundred names guessable, which is what the web app’s word-part quizzes train (thorwhalen/kodokan#65).

The same part is spelled differently depending on its position: Japanese rendaku voices the first consonant of a non-initial part, so koshi (hip) becomes goshi in Harai-goshi but stays koshi in Koshi-guruma; likewise kari/gari, kuruma/guruma, kake/gake, kaeshi/gaeshi. Those variants are one part. A part is keyed by its glossary entry, and a name token maps to it when it is the entry’s primary romaji or one of its declared alternate readings (the glossary’s alts):

>>> glossary = {
...     "goshi": {"alts": ["koshi"]},
...     "guruma": {"alts": ["kuruma"]},
...     "harai": {"alts": ["barai"]},
...     "gaeshi": {"alts": []},
... }
>>> index = variant_index(glossary)
>>> [index.get(t) for t in ("koshi", "goshi", "barai", "kaeshi", "nage")]
['goshi', 'goshi', 'harai', None, None]
>>> parts_of_name("Koshi-guruma", index)
['goshi', 'guruma']

Variants are declared, not guessed: rendaku is regular enough to suggest a variant but not to merge one silently (devoicing de, “advancing”, gives te, “hand”, a different word). So unmatched_tokens() reports the name tokens no part claims, with the part they are a rendaku variant of, for a person to add as an alts entry:

>>> unmatched_tokens({"kaeshiwaza": "Kaeshi-waza"}, glossary)
{'kaeshi': 'gaeshi', 'waza': None}

word_throws() derives part -> techniques by tokenizing the names, rather than trusting a hand-kept list (which had missed Koshi-guruma under goshi), and part_stats() counts the distinct techniques per part. MIN_TECHNIQUES is the threshold for a part to be offered in the quizzes; its docstring records why.

kodokan.vocab.MIN_TECHNIQUES = 3#

A part is offered in the word-part quizzes when at least this many distinct techniques use it. Chosen from the statistics of the 68 nage-waza (2026-11-09, kodokan#65): 23 parts are shared by >=2 techniques, 20 by >=3, 16 by >=4, 13 by >=5. A part shared by only two techniques (obi, sumi, wakare) is a pair to memorize, not a pattern to spot, while 3 keeps seoi (Seoi-nage, Ippon-seoi-nage, Seoi-otoshi), one of the four parts the owner named as the point of the exercise; 4 would drop it. So 3.

spelling -> part key for every primary key and declared alternate reading.
